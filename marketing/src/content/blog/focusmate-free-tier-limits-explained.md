## Why the free tier matters

Focusmate popularized matched 1:1 video focus sessions. Plenty of people start on the free plan, build a habit, then hit a wall: the free allowance only covers a few sessions per week. That is enough to test the format. It is not always enough for daily deep work, exam revision, or freelance billable blocks.

This post explains the friction — not to trash Focusmate — and what to do when the weekly cap shows up. For the concept behind why a partner helps you start, read [our guide to body doubling](https://refocus.co.in/body-doubling).

## What the free plan usually limits

Always verify current details on [Focusmate’s pricing page](https://www.focusmate.com/pricing/). As of common public positioning, the free tier has covered **three sessions per week**. Paid plans raise that ceiling and unlock more flexibility.

Practical effects of a low weekly cap:

- You ration sessions (“save them for hard days”) and skip the easy days that actually build the habit.
- Mid-week you fall back to solo work — the exact state that was failing before.
- You treat the tool as a scarce resource instead of a daily container.

If cameras-on accountability is what makes Focusmate work for you, the product is still excellent. The question is only whether the free allotment matches how often you need to sit down.

## What to do when you hit the cap

1. **Upgrade** if the community and format already work and the cost is fine.
2. **Alternate tools** on free days: library, cafe, or another matched room.
3. **Change the ritual**, not just the app: shorter 25-minute blocks on solo days so you still start.
4. **Compare free tiers honestly** — some alternatives have no weekly session cap during a free period; others are Discord lobbies that look free but cost attention.

## Keeping the habit without gaming the calendar

The goal is not “maximize free sessions.” It is **showing up**. A useful pattern:

- Book the same local time four or five days a week when you can.
- If you only get three free sessions elsewhere, use them on your hardest tasks and use a quieter free room (or a friend on a shared call) for the other days.
- Write the goal in one line before the session starts — same script every time.

[CHADD’s ADHD overview](https://chadd.org/) and the [CDC ADHD pages](https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/) are useful if task initiation is the real blocker; body doubling is one environmental fix among several, not a diagnosis.

## Focusmate is still a strong choice for many people

Credit where due: a large 24/7 community, clear session structure, and years of iteration. If cameras-on accountability is your lever and you are happy to pay past the free tier, stay. Looking for alternatives is about fit — free caps, mic pressure, session lengths — not about declaring a winner forever.

Check Focusmate’s [how it works](https://www.focusmate.com/how-it-works/) docs when rules change, and re-read any alternative’s pricing before you migrate a habit. Tools change; your need for a protected focus block usually does not.

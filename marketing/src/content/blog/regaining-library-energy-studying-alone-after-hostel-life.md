## Missing the Energy of Hostel Life

After months or years of living in a hostel, surrounded by study buddies and a constant buzz of focused energy, returning home can feel isolating for students and remote learners. Whether you're preparing for a major exam like the SAT or just tackling your coursework, the transition from communal study spaces to solo study sessions can leave you feeling adrift. Luckily, you can recreate that library energy in your own home by incorporating strategies that mimic the benefits of studying with others while respecting your need for quiet.

## Quiet Company vs. Conversation

First, it's essential to understand that feeling lonely doesn't always equate to a desire for conversation. After years of working in a vibrant environment, the shift to solitude can feel stark and disheartening. However, you might not need more social interaction; instead, you may just be craving quiet co-presence. This is where the idea of body doubling comes into play. By studying alongside someone—even virtually—you can tap into that beneficial energy without requiring constant conversation.

## Creating a Virtual Study Environment

One of the easiest ways to simulate the library or hostel energy is through virtual coworking. Using platforms that offer study rooms or body doubling sessions allows you to work alongside others, even if you're physically alone. This shared focus can be incredibly motivating. You can look for options like online study rooms or communities that facilitate this kind of engagement. For a deeper dive into how this can help, check out [our guide to virtual coworking](https://refocus.co.in/virtual-coworking).

Additionally, consider setting specific time blocks for studying. Choose a time of day when you were most productive in the hostel—perhaps mid-afternoon—and dedicate that time to focused work. These time blocks can be enhanced by using the Pomodoro technique, where you study intensely for 25 minutes, followed by a 5-minute break. This method can help mimic the rhythm and urgency of hostel life.

## Establishing Routine with Online Tools

To maintain a sense of accountability, consider using online tools that allow you to share your study goals and progress with a small group. Platforms like **Refocus** offer video calls where you can study together silently. This setup can help you stay on track while also providing the subtle encouragement of having someone else in the same situation.

It's also helpful to establish norms for your study sessions. Perhaps you agree to start and end your studies together or check in briefly at the start to share your goals for that session. This routine can create a comforting structure reminiscent of hostel life.

## Finding Your Focus Space

Creating a designated study area in your home can significantly impact your productivity. Ideally, your study space should be quiet and free from distractions, much like the library or your hostel room during exam time. Here are a few practical tips:
- **Declutter your space**: A tidy area helps minimize distractions.
- **Set up comfortable seating**: Invest in a good chair or cushion to make your study area inviting.
- **Use noise-canceling headphones**: Even if your environment is quiet, headphones can help you create a personal bubble of focus.
- **Incorporate ambient noise**: If silence is too loud, consider background noise. There are many apps and websites that offer ambient sounds or white noise that can help you concentrate.

## The Importance of Regular Check-Ins

Don't underestimate the power of check-ins, even if they're brief. Whether you text a friend or share goals in a group chat, keeping others in the loop can provide a sense of connection. As highlighted by the [CDC's insights on social connectedness](https://www.cdc.gov/emotional-wellbeing/social-connectedness/index.htm), fostering connections—even through small gestures—can significantly enhance your emotional well-being.

Having regular check-ins helps to stave off the feelings of isolation that can come with extended solo study sessions. You can also consider setting up a weekly coffee or study chat with a friend just to break the silence, even if the main focus remains on your studies.

## Embrace the Journey

Transitioning back to studying at home after the lively environment of a hostel can be challenging, but it's not insurmountable. By actively seeking out ways to mimic that library energy, you can create an enriching study routine that embraces both focus and companionship—even from a distance. Remember, it's okay to feel lonely at times; acknowledge it and use these strategies to foster a more connected studying experience.

For additional insights on combating loneliness while studying alone, consider checking out this [resource on loneliness](https://www.mind.org.uk/information-support/tips-for-everyday-living/loneliness/about-loneliness/), which provides practical tips for managing feelings of isolation.

Finally, while adjusting may take time, patience and persistence in creating your new study environment will lead to fruitful results.

## Understanding the Challenge of Verbal Prep

Preparing for the GRE or GMAT verbal sections can be daunting, especially with the constant temptation of distractions in the form of your phone or social media. With verbal exams requiring deep comprehension and critical thinking, it’s crucial to create a focused study environment. This post outlines practical strategies to help you maximize your focus during study sessions and avoid the common pitfall of scrolling through your phone instead of studying.

## Set Clear Goals for Each Session

Before starting your study session, define what you aim to achieve. For instance, instead of a vague goal like "study verbal section," set a specific target such as "complete three reading comprehension passages and review mistakes." This clarity helps to keep you focused and provides a sense of accomplishment when you complete your goals. 

### Suggested Goal Setting Method:
1. **Identify Topics**: Break down the verbal section into smaller topics (e.g., vocabulary, reading comprehension, sentence correction).
2. **Allocate Time**: Assign a specific time frame for each topic (e.g., 25 minutes for reading comprehension).
3. **Stay Flexible**: If you finish early, consider reviewing what you’ve learned or moving to the next topic.

## Use the Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique involves studying in short, focused bursts followed by brief breaks. This method not only maintains your concentration but also creates a sense of urgency that can deter distractions. Here’s how you can implement it:
- **Set a Timer for 25 Minutes**: Focus exclusively on your verbal prep during this time.
- **Take a 5-Minute Break**: Step away from your study space, stretch, or hydrate.
- **Repeat for Four Sessions**: After completing four study sessions, take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

This technique can help you engage fully during your study blocks, making it less likely that you will drift into scrolling on your phone.

## Create a Distraction-Free Environment

Your environment plays a significant role in your focus. Make sure to:
- **Clear Your Study Space**: Remove unnecessary items from your desk that might catch your attention.
- **Use Apps to Limit Distractions**: Consider apps like Forest or Freedom that block distracting websites and notifications during your study sessions.
- **Keep Your Phone Out of Reach**: Place your phone in another room or in a drawer to reduce temptation to check it.

## Incorporate Body Doubling Techniques

Studying alongside someone else can be beneficial for maintaining focus and accountability. If your study partner is working on similar subjects, you can motivate each other without speaking. If physical presence isn’t possible, virtual body doubling can be a great alternative. Tools like Refocus provide a platform for this kind of structured study environment.

- **Join Study Rooms**: Engage with others in an online study room for shared focus and accountability.
- **Schedule Regular Sessions**: Make it a habit to meet at the same time with your study partner, whether online or in person.

## Practice Active Reading Techniques

To effectively prepare for the verbal sections, you must develop active reading habits:
- **Summarize After Reading**: After each passage or sentence, summarize what you read in your own words. This reinforces comprehension and retention.
- **Annotate Passages**: Take notes in the margins of your practice passages, marking key points, transitions, and vocabulary.
- **Discuss with Peers**: If possible, discuss complex passages with study partners. Explaining concepts to someone else can solidify your understanding.

## Evaluate Your Progress Regularly

Set aside time weekly to assess your understanding and progress:
- **Take Practice Tests**: Simulate test conditions with practice tests available on the [official GRE website](https://www.ets.org/gre.html) or [GMAT site](https://www.mba.com/exams/gmat).
- **Review Mistakes**: Focus on incorrect answers to understand where you went wrong. This will help you avoid making the same mistakes in future sessions.

## Conclusion

By applying these techniques, you can transform your GRE or GMAT verbal preparation into focused, productive sessions that steer clear of distractions. Remember, the goal is to create an environment and a mindset conducive to deep focus while actively engaging with the material. The more you practice these strategies, the more natural they will become, allowing you to maximize your study efficiency without the lure of phone scrolling.

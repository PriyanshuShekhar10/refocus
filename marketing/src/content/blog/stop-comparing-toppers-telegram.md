## The Draw of Telegram Groups

In the life of a competitive exam aspirant—whether preparing for UPSC, JEE Main, or NEET—Telegram groups can be a double-edged sword. On one hand, they create a sense of community and can provide valuable resources. On the other, they often become breeding grounds for comparison and self-doubt. You might find yourself scrolling through posts, seeing others share their impressive mock test scores or unique study techniques, and suddenly feel inadequate or distracted from your own goals.

## Why Comparing Yourself Harms Your Progress

It’s easy to fall into the trap of believing that everyone else has the perfect study plan or the perfect grasp of difficult subjects like Physics or Calculus. This can lead to a few harmful outcomes:

1. **Loss of Focus**: Constantly comparing yourself to others pulls your attention away from your study schedule. Instead of concentrating on your preparation, you might be fixated on someone else's result or methodology.
2. **Increased Anxiety**: Peer comparison can amplify anxiety levels. If you feel you're falling short of what others are achieving, it can lead to a sense of hopelessness about your own preparation.
3. **Disruption of Self-Efficacy**: Seeing others excel can cause you to doubt your own capabilities, leading to demotivation and a lack of confidence.

## Establish Your Own Study Plan

Instead of tracking what others are doing, focus on creating a study plan that works specifically for you. Here’s how:

### 1. Set Clear, Personal Goals

Start by assessing where you currently stand in your preparation relative to the syllabus for your exam. For instance, if you’re getting ready for the NEET exam, list the chapters in Biology, Chemistry, and Physics. Decide how much time you will allocate to each subject based on your strengths and weaknesses.

### 2. Create a Realistic Schedule

Draft a daily or weekly schedule that incorporates study sessions, breaks, and even time for relaxing activities. Consider using tools like time-blocking to help manage your sessions better, allowing you to adjust based on your progress. Resources like [NTA's NEET site](https://neet.nta.nic.in/) can provide you with a clear syllabus breakdown that may aid in planning.

### 3. Use Active Study Techniques

Instead of merely reading or highlighting notes, try engaging techniques like self-quizzing, flashcards, or teaching concepts to someone else. This active recall can help reinforce your learning and improve retention.

### 4. Regularly Reflect on Your Progress

Take time weekly to evaluate what you've accomplished. This can be as simple as checking off topics on your list or taking a mock test. If you're preparing for the JEE Main, check [NTA's JEE Main site](https://jeemain.nta.nic.in/) for mock tests and past papers to gauge your standing.

## Find Your Study Buddy, Not Your Comparison Rival

While Telegram can trigger negative comparisons, it doesn’t have to be all bad. Consider using it to find a study buddy instead of a rival. Discussing challenges or studying together (even virtually) can help you stay accountable and focused.

If you feel overwhelmed, you might also consider platforms like Refocus, which facilitate virtual study sessions with peers. This creates a more productive environment without the burden of constant comparison.

## Conclusion

Ultimately, your journey through competitive exam preparation should be about you—your goals, your progress, and your personal growth. Embrace your path, set clear objectives, and return to your unique study plan whenever you feel the urge to compare yourself to the top scorers in those Telegram groups. Focus on what you can control, and remember that consistency and self-belief will get you far, whether you're aiming for UPSC, CAT, or GATE exams. Transform that anxiety into actionable study sessions, and keep your eyes on your own track.  

For further information on exam strategies and insights, you can check out the [UPSC official site](https://upsc.gov.in/) or resources for various competitive exams.
